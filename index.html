Current venture

Caravans of the Copper Road

A merchant-first living-world trade simulation

A world of settlements, merchants, raiders and hired blades that keeps moving whether or not you do. You get rich by reading roads, prices, timing and risk better than anyone else. The first goal is a small simulation laboratory: three towns, a handful of roads, and one question. Does it tell stories on its own?

Progress

  1. Sep 29, 2026

    Sellswords. Merchants now hire guards for roads they've heard are dangerous, and with a few blades along they dare the short way through the forest. Sellswords have strength, agility, discipline, awareness and nerve, and they grow slowly from what they live through. A rank comes from deeds, and it raises whichever stat those deeds trained: forest ambushes sharpen the eye, holding the line steadies it. Survive enough fights in the Blackpine and you become Forestwise. Fight beside the same partner often enough and you become a Trusted Pair. Their gear is made by the town smiths from real tools, timber and cloth, and it keeps its own record. A spear that has seen enough fights earns a tier and, in time, a name, like "the Blackpine Thorn". When a guard falls, the outlaws take the gear, so a famous blade can end up in a bandit's hands. Charms work if people believe in them, and only the laboratory knows which few are real relics. The one scute coat, made from the great tortoise's shed shell, is on someone's back.

  2. Sep 28, 2026

    Build step E complete: raiders. When times are hard, out-of-work labourers and starving miners take to the hills and join outlaw bands in the Blackpine hollows, the fens and the caves under the Saddle. The bands watch the roads that pay, demand tolls, fall on caravans and creep into camps at night. Hired hands die, merchants are held for ransom, and letters taken from the lord's post never arrive. The bands have to eat, too: they fence their loot cheap in town, bury their coin, and when winter starves them they raid a village or break up and go home. News of the roads spreads like news of prices, so a road keeps its bad name after the band has moved on, and Lord Aldric sends patrols where he hears of trouble. Surprise floods and snows strand travellers, who make camp; one camp became a waystation, "Rook's Rest". On the map you can see the hideouts, which roads the bands are watching, and where they struck.

  3. Sep 28, 2026

    Lord Aldric wakes up. The lord now has a temper and moods, and he spends his treasury instead of sitting on it. When a town goes hungry he has criers offer a good price for grain brought there, and merchants who hear of it carry the bread. He buys up glutted cloth and timber, throws festivals, and saves up for works that last: granaries, new fields, new houses, workshops. His spending keeps the Mint busy, which keeps Copperford's miners paid. His steward, meanwhile, has started helping himself. The laboratory has a new panel showing what the lord is thinking and why.

  4. Sep 28, 2026

    Build step D complete: merchants. News now travels at road speed: inns keep price lists, rumours get a little wrong in the retelling, and the lord's post riders carry letters. Five trading houses buy, carry and sell real goods on what they've heard, which is never quite what's true, so they sometimes arrive to find the famine over or a rival already there. Houses grow, get "borrowed" from by the lord, go broke and are replaced, and a lucky peddler can save up, trade the pack for a wagon and found a house. Saltmouth's ships now pay coin for exports. With trade, fewer people starve. In the laboratory, caravans roll across the map, and you can open any merchant to see what they know and why they chose their trade.

  5. Sep 27, 2026

    Build step C complete: coin. The Copper Road now runs on copper marks struck at the Copperford Mint, with twelve verdigris bits to the mark (scutes, shed plates of the great tortoise's shell, will come later). Households buy what they can afford, Lord Aldric pays his guards and sends the Crown its due, and coin with nothing to buy gets buried under floorboards. Famine can now kill, but people turn to the land, move to fed towns and have children, so the region settles instead of dying out. The map tells you what you're hovering over, including snowbound passes and flooded fords.

  6. Sep 26, 2026

    Build step B complete: the economy. Every town now has named people with trades: farmers, miners, smiths, weavers, shepherds and more. What they make and eat sets the price of nine goods. Harvests come in autumn, firewood and medicine get dear in winter, and when a smith dies an apprentice takes up the hammer. There are no merchants yet, so the towns slowly starve for want of trade. The laboratory's new opportunity board shows what fixing that is worth. The spec (v0.2.2) now includes traveller livelihoods and a living map where camps can grow into towns.

  7. Sep 26, 2026

    Build step A complete. The simulation core is running: a world clock with seasons and daylight, the road network (floods in spring, a pass snowed shut in winter), and a dozen wayfarers who choose their roads by speed and nerve. A hundred days take about 20 milliseconds, and the same seed gives the same world in Node and in your browser. Watch it run in the laboratory.

  8. Sep 26, 2026

    Spec v0.2.1: you inherit the family stall and the family debt. Lord Aldric of Kingscross has moods, wants, and an heir waiting. The tortoise already has three nicknames and they're competing.

  9. Sep 26, 2026

    Design spec v0.2: the player is now a person in one town who learns about the world by letter. Seasons, a port to the Outside, a copper mint and a wealth levy open up the economy. Every citizen works. Raiders fence and bury treasure. And a market town may ride on a giant tortoise.
